## Copy

**Body.**

What happens when a regulated financial institution needs to move fast on compliance, but won't sacrifice the legal structure that makes it defensible?

Our first banking cohort went live in April. One institution in particular moved through our onboarding in weeks, not quarters. They had a specific problem: data governance across legacy systems and new EU obligations. They needed a *verifiable* audit trail, not a checkbox.

They shipped it. With signed compliance documentation attached to every decision. No delays waiting for legal review cycles. No ambiguity about what was processed, why, and under which framework.

That's the difference when compliance isn't an afterthought. It's the *architecture*.

Early movers in regulated industries aren't looking for features. They're looking for something you can sign and defend in a regulatory meeting. Something that turns "how did you handle that data" from a risk conversation into a documented answer.
