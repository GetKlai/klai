**Body.**

What does a month of live feedback actually teach you?

We shipped Klai in late April. A hundred early users. Real work, real data, real friction. And the thing that surprised us most wasn't what people built with it, though that's been remarkable. It was what we got wrong about what they'd need.

We thought speed would be the primary win. But the hundred voices told us something different: they came for the control. For *transparency*. For the ability to audit what's happening inside the model without a black box between them and their output.

A compliance officer asked us in week two if they could trace every training data source. A product lead wanted to know exactly which parameters shaped a decision. An in-house counsel wanted contractual guarantees about data residency. These weren't edge cases. They were the pattern.

So we listened. We doubled down on what actually mattered to the people doing the work: structure. Accountability. EU processing by default, not exception. The kind of *foundational* choices that don't look flashy but let people sleep at night.

One month in, we're still small. Still learning. But we know now that our job isn't to be the fastest. It's to be the one they can trust.

That's what the first hundred taught us.
